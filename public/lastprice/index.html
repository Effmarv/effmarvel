Case study · Nigeria

LastPrice.ng: your shop and bookings in one link

A free shop link for Nigerian small sellers. Customers see prices, pick what they want, and the order lands in the seller's WhatsApp. No website and no app to download.

The problem

In Nigeria, a huge amount of small business happens on WhatsApp and Instagram. Sellers of fashion, cakes, skincare, phones and hair services answer the same question all day: "How much? What's your last price?"

Building a website is expensive and slow, and most customers won't download another app or wait for a heavy page to load on mobile data.

Key decisions

Free forever, not a free trialUp to 10 items free with no end date, so any seller can start today. Pro (₦2,500 a month) unlocks unlimited items, more photos, a customer list and full stats.
WhatsApp is the checkoutOrders arrive in the seller's WhatsApp, where they already talk to customers. Payment is agreed in the chat by transfer or cash, so there's nothing new to learn.
Light enough for slow dataBuilt for everyday Android phones on patchy connections, because that's what most customers actually use.
A founding offer to start momentumThe first 100 shops keep 20 free items forever, which rewards early sellers for taking a chance on it.

What I built

  • A shop link and QR poster for each seller, ready to share on WhatsApp status, Instagram or a market stall.
  • Items and services in one place, so a salon or barber can take booking requests as easily as a boutique takes orders.
  • Orders straight to WhatsApp, with an order number and a total, so nothing gets lost in the chat.
  • Seller tools on Pro: customer list, order history, best sellers, promo prices and their own colours.
  • Weekly stats so sellers can see what's working.

Results so far

LastPrice.ng is live at lastprice.ng, open to sellers across Nigeria.

Numbers to add: shops created, orders sent and founding spots taken.

What I learned

LastPrice taught me to build around the tools people already trust. Sellers didn't need a new place to talk to customers; they needed their prices in front of them before the chat even started.
