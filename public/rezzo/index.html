Rezzo: bookings for your business, in your pocket

A booking page, deposits, reminders, a shop and a diary for UK salons, barbers, nail techs and lash artists. One link in their Instagram bio, a flat £9 a month, and no commission on any booking.

The problem

Most small UK beauty businesses run their bookings through Instagram DMs and WhatsApp. That means hours of back-and-forth about prices and times, double bookings, and no-shows that wipe out a day's earnings.

The existing tools were built for bigger salons. They charge per staff member, take a cut of each booking, or push clients into downloading an app and making an account. For a braider working alone, that's too much cost and too much friction for her clients.

Key decisions

A flat price with no commission£9 a month for a solo operator, £15 for a team regardless of size, and 0% commission. Businesses keep every pound of every booking, which is easy to explain and hard for marketplace-style competitors to match.
No accounts for clientsClients book from one link with just a name and mobile number. Every extra step loses bookings, so there's no app and no sign-up on the client side.
Instant booking on by defaultBusy owners can't approve requests in the middle of an appointment, so new businesses accept bookings instantly. They can still switch to approval if they want.
Deposits paid straight to the businessPayments go through Stripe Connect into the owner's own bank account, so deposits cut no-shows without Rezzo ever holding their money.
Mobile first, everywhereThe owners run their business from their phone, and so do their clients. Every screen is designed at phone width first.

What I built

  • A booking page per business with services, categories, styles, team, work photos, opening hours, policies, Google reviews and a month calendar, plus a Book now button for the business's own website and printable QR posters.
  • A home-screen app for every shop: clients add the business to their phone with its own logo and book again in one tap.
  • Payments: deposits or full price, discount codes, and yearly plans with Bacs Direct Debit, all on Stripe.
  • iPhone and Android apps for owners: diary, clients, staff rota, breaks, push notifications, a fingerprint or face lock, and update prompts when a new version is out.
  • Solo or team mode: a simple setup for one person, with team tools (roles, rota, a front desk view) only when there's more than one operator.
  • Walk-ins: a live queue that clients join by scanning a QR code at the door.
  • Keeping clients: memberships with member discounts, a loyalty card, rebooking reminders, review requests, and email and WhatsApp confirmations.
  • A shop so businesses can sell products alongside services.
  • Easy sign-in with Apple or Google.
  • AI where it saves time: it writes the business bio, drafts support replies and writes news updates.
  • Support and admin: an in-app help chat, a team inbox, and account deletion that meets app store rules.

Results so far

Rezzo is live at rezzo.co.uk, with iPhone and Android apps for business owners. From the first line of code to a working product with payments, apps and emails took about four days and 95 shipped changes.

Launch is underway now. I'll add real numbers here (businesses, bookings, no-shows saved) as they come in.

What I learned

The decisions mattered more than the features. Choosing a flat price, no client accounts and instant booking shaped everything else, and they all came from picturing one person: a braider with her hands full.
